# Round 3, verbatim

## Answers on 34 and 35, decisions, and the last three questions (Claude)

**34, the real answer.** The blast itself does not give the island a tsunami. A surface burst in the harbor throws up a local wave that shrinks with distance and is capped by the shallow harbor, so Catalina would get a swell, not an inundation. The damaging wave comes from a submarine landslide at the shelf edge (the Palos Verdes slide complex, which the state already uses as a tsunami source for the LA County hazard maps we are using). The one stretch in the premise is the trigger: a surface burst couples only a small share of its energy into the ground, far below a large earthquake. Accept that as the premise and keep one knob: slide volume sets wave height per cove. Everything else follows from data already computed: arrival about 8 minutes from the shelf break, 15 from the port, sound at under 2 minutes. The slide can lag the flash by an unknown minute or two, so the player sees the flash and never gets a countdown.

**35, EMP.** Island-wide electronics damage needs a high-altitude burst (above roughly 30 km), which blankets a region the size of several states. A low airburst or surface burst produces damaging EMP only within a few km of the fireball. The two burst types also split the core mechanics: a surface burst gives fallout and ground shock (the slide trigger) but almost no distant EMP; a low airburst over the city gives neither fallout nor ground shock, so it kills both mechanics. Conclusion: base scenario is a single surface burst at the port, no EMP. A high-altitude precursor burst can be a later modifier; it would also kill phones, GPS receivers, modern outboard ignitions and the grid controls while old mechanical diesels keep running.

Decided, not asked: solo default is one life; a "lead" mode passes control to a companion; multiplayer is one life each. Character creation stays minimal: age band, fitness, two skills, a kit from a budget, and the one Y/N. Designated shelters where people wait with supplies count toward rescue scoring.

**Last three**
67. Blast timing: fixed clock time per seed but unknown to the player, with the ferry or charter choice setting how long you have been ashore? Or a known prep window? Default: unknown, within the first afternoon or evening.
68. Money: three set budgets as difficulty (Oregon Trail style) spent on kit and transport before departure; no money matters after the flash. Default: yes.
69. Single severity knob: slide volume only; warhead yield fixed at a strategic class, wind tape already guarantees fallout. Default: yes.

No other questions. With these answered or defaulted, the representation spec starts.
