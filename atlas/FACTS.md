# Facts for Escape from Catalina

A digest of four sourced research passes made on 2026-10-17, plus the atlas's own measurements. The full
sheets with every source URL and a confidence per line are in `atlas/research/`. Where sources disagree the
range is given. Nothing here is invented; the invented parts of the game are listed separately in GAPS.md.

Rules that shape play: anchoring only 100 yd outside a mooring field, 14 days max; unattended vessels prohibited
Nov to Mar; Harbor Patrol off-season 8:00 to 17:30 at Isthmus, Cherry and Fourth of July only, outer coves
checked twice a day, nobody on the water overnight; shoreboat winter hours 8:00 to 17:00. Cruise ships use three
federal anchorage circles off Avalon (1,350 ft radius, 30 to 45 fathoms) and tender in. The Pebbly Beach
freight dock takes ~90% of the island's freight; the landing craft Catalina Provider (150 ft, 12 passengers plus
trucks) can land on any beach and was tested as an evacuation vessel.

## How many boats are there

- Avalon, October 2025: 1,264 vessels moored over the month, 5,209 mooring-nights, so about 170 boats on
  moorings on an average night (weekends heavier). October 2017: 1,417 moored and 126 anchored. Whole year 2022:
  14,076 boats, 58,132 nights. Off-season: November 2024 626 moored, January 2025 307.
- Two Harbors: only a June 2023 report exists: 1,428 moored, 157 anchored, 5,712 people aboard. Buccaneer Days
  (early October, 1 to 4 October in 2026) sells the coves out.
- The atlas counted hulls on USDA aerial imagery from 11 May 2022 (a Wednesday); see the Boats tab. Chop defeats
  the detector outside sheltered water, so those are lower bounds for the calm inner harbors only.
- Boats based here: Catalina Express fleet of 8 (Jet Cat Express 381 pax 145 ft; Catalina Jet 449 pax 144 ft;
  Starship Express 300 pax 134 ft; Cat Express 300 pax 97 ft; four 149-pax monohulls of 95 to 100 ft; 26 to 33 kt);
  Catalina Flyer (118 ft, 600 pax, Newport, one round trip a day); Avalon Freight: landing craft Catalina Provider
  150 ft, tug Lucy Franco 69 ft 1,500 hp, two deck barges and a ramp barge carrying a 90,000 gal water tank, Mon
  to Fri from San Pedro at 16:00, 3 h crossing; the Cyclone speedboat Avalon to Two Harbors runs May to September
  only; Baywatch Avalon and Baywatch Isthmus 32 ft rescue boats, 30 kt, 1,000 gpm fire pumps; two Avalon Harbor
  Patrol outboard boats; a Sheriff's boat at Two Harbors and one at Avalon; two 24 ft shoreboat inflatables
  (19 to 21 pax); USC's Miss Christi (42 pax) three runs a week plus a fleet of ~10 skiffs 11 to 25 ft; Island
  Company glass-bottom boats and semi-subs; Two Harbors shoreboats.

## Piers, docks, fuel

- Green Pleasure Pier ~300 ft, harbor office, 2-ton hoist, end float being replaced (60 by 20 ft). Cabrillo Mole
  46 by 400 ft (1968), three 100 ft floats with 30 ft alongside, the ferry terminal; a $24.5M rebuild runs July
  2026 to December 2027. Pebbly Beach: freight dock, heliport (44 by 44 ft pad at 6 ft), SCE plant and desal at
  ~25 ft elevation. Two Harbors pier: 12 ft at the end, fuel dock on the east side, ferries dock here. Cat Harbor:
  dinghy dock. USC Big Fisherman Cove: dock, pier, day and night helipad, hyperbaric chamber.
- Avalon fuel dock (Dion and Sons, 2 Casino Way): diesel, mid-grade and 93 octane ethanol-free; four underground
  tanks of unpublished size; 8:00 to 17:00 or 18:00. Two Harbors fuel dock: gas, diesel, water, 8:00 to 16:00
  year-round. Road fuel: one self-serve station at 56 Pebbly Beach Rd (about $7 a gallon) and a Two Harbors
  station; propane from Chet's Hardware, Pebbly Beach Building Supply and the Two Harbors Parts and Supply
  Warehouse. The airport sells no fuel.
- Southern California Edison, Pebbly Beach: two 125,000 gal diesel tanks (30-day reserve), three usable 30,000 gal
  propane tanks (90,000 gal, shared with the town's gas customers), two 10,000 gal urea tanks. The island burns
  about 2 million gal of diesel a year (280 tanker loads) and 0.8 to 1.0 million gal of propane; ~310 barge
  trips a year; the longest gap between fuel deliveries in 2017 to 2021 was 5 days.

## Power, water, sewage, communications

- Isolated grid, no mainland tie. Pebbly Beach Generating Station: six diesel units totalling 9.3 MW (built
  1958 to 1995), 23 propane microturbines (1.5 MW nameplate, about 1 MW usable, limited to 30% of load), a
  1 MW / 7.5 MWh sodium-sulfur battery. Average load 3.3 MW, peak 5.5 to 6 MW. Three 12 kV circuits (Hi Line,
  Interior, Wrigley) feed the whole island, Two Harbors included; there is no second plant.
- Water (SCE): two reservoirs, 10 wells, 13 storage tanks, two desalination plants and over 45 miles of pipe for
  about 2,000 accounts and 4,100 year-round people. Middle Ranch (Thompson) Reservoir holds 1,065 acre-feet
  (it fell to 127 in 2016). Avalon drinks three shallow wells in Middle Canyon plus desal: the two plants make up
  to 230,400 gal a day, about 40% of the town's water, into three tanks holding 375,000 gal. Avalon demand is
  about 200,000 gal a day in winter and up to 800,000 in summer. Two Harbors is fed from the wells by the Two
  Harbors Pipeline through the "Million Gallon Tank" and has no desal. Avalon toilets flush with seawater from a
  separate city system; some hydrants run saltwater. Every campground except Parsons has potable water.
- Sewage: Avalon plant at Pebbly Beach, 1.2 million gal a day, ocean outfall; Two Harbors plant 0.25 mi south of
  Isthmus Cove, 57,000 gal a day, 270,000 gal holding pond, spray irrigation.
- Communications: three cell sites (Mt Ada for Avalon, Blackjack, Isthmus) shared by the carriers; coverage
  reaches 4 to 5 miles offshore; a 15-hour island-wide cell outage happened in February 2024. AT&T copper
  landlines stay up on central-office generators. The mainland link is microwave (a buried fiber is funded for
  2028). Cable internet in Avalon; satellite elsewhere.
- Trash: Avalon's landfill at Pebbly Beach has about two years of capacity; everything outside Avalon is barged.

## People

- Residents: Avalon 3,460 (2020 census), 3,447 estimated for 2026; the island about 4,100 year-round; Two
  Harbors 150 to 300 depending on the source. Staff: Catalina Island Company about 300 (seasonal April to
  October in dormitory housing), Conservancy over 100, Camp Emerald Bay 150 in summer only. Avalon K-12 has 443
  students; Two Harbors' one-room school closed in 2014.
- Visitors: 1.21 million arrivals in 2025. October 2025: 98,686 arrivals, 55% by ferry (2,308 of them at Two
  Harbors, about 75 a day), 35,542 cruise passengers on 11 calls (about 3,200 per call), 5,209 private boaters,
  3,016 by air. Hotel occupancy 67%, short-term rentals 39% of about 330 listings.
- The week of the trip: Monday 12 October 2026 Serenade of the Seas (2,100 to 2,500 passengers) 08:00 to 18:00;
  Wednesday 14 October Ovation of the Seas (4,182); Thursday 15 October Disney Wonder (2,713). Catalina is a
  tender-only port: passengers land at the Mole and the Pleasure Pier in boatloads.
- A fair estimate for a mid-October Monday at noon: Avalon 6,000 to 9,500 people with a ship in, 4,500 to 6,500
  without; Two Harbors 250 to 600 (residents, about 75 ferry arrivals, campers, moored boaters, USC staff). The
  summer camps are closed in October.
- Evacuation precedent: the 2007 Island Fire moved about 3,800 people off by ferry overnight; Hurricane Hilary
  2023 brought an evacuation warning and a closed harbor; the July 2025 tsunami advisory closed Pebbly Beach Road
  and the waterfront but ordered no evacuation.

## Emergency services and medicine

- Baywatch Avalon (office on the Pleasure Pier) and Baywatch Isthmus: county lifeguard paramedics, two on duty
  around the clock at each end (2005 figure), 32 ft rescue boats, yellow 4WD trucks; split at Twin Rocks and
  Ben Weston. Avalon Fire Department: 11 full-time plus 30 reserves, three ~20-year-old engines and an 80 ft quint,
  no paramedics. LA County Fire Station 55 (Avalon Canyon, Engine 55 and Patrol 55) and Station 155 (Two
  Harbors, on-call). Sheriff's Avalon Station at 215 Sumner Ave, 24 hours, with a boat at each end; a reserve
  search and rescue team with a West End squad. Avalon Harbor Patrol about 12 full-time officers, two boats, EMTs.
  No Coast Guard on the island: boats come from Los Angeles-Long Beach, helicopters from Ventura.
- Catalina Island Medical Center, 100 Falls Canyon Rd: 12 licensed beds (4 skilled nursing), standby emergency
  department staffed 24/7 with UC Irvine physicians, CT scanner, lab, telemedicine; about 250 patients a year flown
  out; no helipad, so air ambulances use the Pebbly Beach pad (where a REACH EC135 crashed on 30 September 2026);
  the building must meet a 2030 seismic deadline. USC hyperbaric chamber at Big Fisherman Cove, 24/7, run by
  volunteers, extension of LAC+USC. Leo's Drugstore is the only pharmacy (owned by the hospital); one dentist.
- Alerts: text "Avalon" to 38276; a siren is tested at noon on the last Friday of the month (continuous means a
  significant threat). The Casino is described as the civil-defense shelter for the year-round population with
  two weeks of food and water (a 2002 book claim, not verified).

## Vehicles and machinery

- Avalon: 1,140 autoettes registered in 2021 (828 residential, 312 non-residential); category counts in 2025:
  1,700 residential permits, 170 business, 125 rental autoettes, 45 interior residential, 450 recreational,
  215 commercial, 120 public-service, 130 interior commercial; "a cap of 800 vehicles, 860 full-size on the
  road"; 25-year waiting list. Autoettes max 130 in long, full-size vehicles max 200 in. Rental carts cannot
  pass the Airport Road gate. City transit COAST: five 5-seat GEM shuttles and two electric vans.
- Conservancy roads: permits, 25 mph, plate-reader gates, OHVs banned. Interior fleets: Island Company H1
  Hummers on vegetable-oil biodiesel (12 passengers), two or three BYD electric 18-seat coaches, the Safari bus;
  Conservancy Wildlands Express vans, Jeeps, a Tundra; Catalina Taxi vans (7) and 4Runners (4), interior shuttle
  at $246 a run; CR&R garbage trucks; SCE service trucks; Connolly-Pacific quarry at Pebbly Beach (22 workers,
  Cat 992D loader, drill and blast, armor stone to 30 tons onto barges) and the Empire Landing quarry.
- Fire apparatus: Avalon FD 2005 Suburban command, 1998 Ford, 2003 Westates, 1988 E-One 80 ft quint; county
  Engine 55 (Type 3), Patrol 55, Engine 155, Patrol 155 (fan-wiki rosters, low confidence).
- Aircraft: Airport in the Sky, 3,000 by 75 ft concrete (rebuilt by Marines 2019), 1,602 ft elevation, open
  08:00 to 17:00, no night operations, 12,500 lb limit, 6 to 10 based aircraft, no fuel; DC-3 freight flights from
  Long Beach; Maverick (ex IEX) helicopters Long Beach to Pebbly Beach in 13 to 15 min, charters to Two Harbors.

## Military, bunkers, mines, caves, hard buildings

- 1864 Union barracks at the Isthmus (now the Isthmus Yacht Club, the island's oldest building). WWII: Coast
  Guard training base at Two Harbors (1,594 acres, ~75 temporary buildings, underground boiler-fuel tanks whose
  fill pipes are still visible); OSS camps at Toyon Bay, Cherry Valley, Gallagher's, White's, Howlands and Emerald
  Bay (189 acres; concrete tanks under the Toyon basketball court); Navy frogmen at Emerald Bay; Camp Cactus
  (Army Signal Corps, 3.5 acres for 500 men, radar station J-41 on Cactus Peak at 1,560 ft, five buildings
  still standing on Conservancy land, concrete floors only in the latrine and motor pool); the airport as an Army
  Air Forces base. Two large underground concrete gun-emplacement bunkers survive on the promontory between
  Cottonwood Cove and Ben Weston Beach (a steep "Ben Weston Bunkers Spur" trail reaches them). No Cold War
  installation and no road tunnel exist; the "cave" below Holly Hill House is an 1890s tunnel stub.
- Mines (USGS records): Black Jack Mine on the NE slope of Black Jack Mountain, 33.38532 -118.40617, a 535 ft
  shaft with levels at 100 to 500 ft and 1,524 m of workings, lead-zinc-silver, worked 1923 to 1927, reported
  sealed and flooded; Renton Mine 2 miles SE of Avalon, 33.32421 -118.30922, five adits and 732 m of workings;
  a Cherry Valley silver mine with a 142 ft drift at 33.45003 -118.51257; 1860s shafts in Fourth of July and Cherry
  valleys; Silver Canyon tunnels; Tongva soapstone quarries by the airport, Empire Landing and Potts Valley.
  Rock quarries at Pebbly Beach (active) and Empire Landing (active since 1949).
- Caves: Blue Cavern Point sea cave (enter one opening, leave by another), Perdition Cave near the Isthmus (room
  for several inflatables), a cave through the Long Point headland, Torqua Cave rock shelter (archaeological),
  many shallow wet and dry caves in the Isthmus headlands.
- Hard buildings: the Casino (reinforced concrete, the height of a 12-story building, 1,154-seat theatre,
  20,000 sq ft ballroom), the hospital, the SCE plant and desal buildings, water tanks, the airport terminal and
  hangar, the Conservancy's Middle Ranch (offices, nursery, field lab, bunkhouse), El Rancho Escondido (16-stall
  stable, main house, bunkhouse, chapel), Camp Emerald Bay (tents for 450, 20 cabins of 8, dining hall), CIMI's
  1928 school quadrangles at Toyon Bay, USC's 30,000 sq ft lab with dorms and dining hall.

## Animals and ecology

- **Bison:** fewer than 80 to about 90 animals (Conservancy permit filings, 2024 to 2026), down from a peak of 527 in
  1987; contraception since 2009; the last calf was born in 2013, the herd is no longer reproducing and will be
  allowed to die out over 20 to 40 years. Range: fenced out of the West End (west of the Isthmus) for 15 years;
  98% of sightings in the central island, concentrated in the grassland basin by the Airport in the Sky and along
  the main road near El Rancho Escondido; they wander into Two Harbors village and interior campgrounds (a camper
  was gored at Little Harbor in 2018). Keep 125 ft; up to 1,800 lb, 35 mph, jumps 6 ft; bulls alone in odd places;
  they use the roads and trails and hold their line.
- **Mule deer:** about 2,040 in the interior (July 2025, 27 per square mile) plus about 150 in Avalon, where
  density is six times higher; the series since 2012 runs 1,227 to 2,619. Removal is under way: a CDFW permit
  (January 2026) for complete removal, professional night shooting over bait beginning in the Middle Ranch area in
  September and October 2026 (about 200 deer, trails closed at night, no firearms within 150 yards of buildings),
  then island-wide through 2032; the final resident hunting season runs 24 October to 18 December 2026. Courts
  rejected the challenge on 28 September 2026.
- **Island fox:** 2,162 (2025), about 10 per square km, federally threatened; 41 deaths in 2025, 18 by vehicles,
  mostly around Avalon; half carry ear tumours from cat mites.
- **Bald eagles:** 8 breeding territories (West End, Empire, Twin Rocks, Rattlesnake, Seal Rocks, Pinnacle Rock,
  Middle Ranch by Thompson Reservoir, Two Harbors above the village); 9 or 10 fledged in 2025. Peregrines: 1 or 2
  pairs (Silver Peak, Lone Tree). Ospreys: none nesting. Ravens take eagle eggs.
- **Other animals:** feral cats 600 to 750 (a 2010 estimate, 70% in the towns), black and Norway rats, house
  mice, an endemic ground squirrel everywhere, Southern Pacific rattlesnakes (more defensive than mainland
  snakes; April to October, on blacktop at night), kingsnakes, the endemic shrew, 10 bat species, honey bees
  and yellow jackets. Goats, pigs, sheep, cattle and blackbuck are gone (pigs by 2004, goats by 2005). Sea
  lions by the hundreds at Seal Rocks south of Avalon; harbor seals; elephant seals occasionally; gray whales in
  winter, blue whales in summer, dolphins year-round. Kelp forests thinned since 2014 and are recovering unevenly.
- **Vegetation (2000 Conservancy map, public ArcGIS service):** coastal sage scrub 38%, island chaparral 29%,
  grassland 19% (35% of the island is invasive annual grass, the fire fuel), bare 9%, developed 1%, island
  woodland (ironwood, island oak, Catalina cherry) only 0.5% on north-facing canyon slopes of Middle and Bullrush
  canyons, riparian woodland 0.3% in the canyon bottoms. About 400 native plant species, 60 plus endemics.
- **Fire:** the 2007 Island Fire burned 4,750 acres above Avalon; nothing large since (17 acres near Black Jack in
  November 2021); 6 of 229 fires in a century were lightning. Grassland burns every 3 to 5 years, shrubland every
  40 to 100; a Santa Ana adds up to 60% to spread; daily fire danger is posted by the Conservancy.
- **Water in the landscape:** Middle Ranch (Thompson) Reservoir held 670 acre-feet on 31 August 2026 against a
  capacity of about 1,054 (full in March 2023, down to 157 in 2016); Stage 1 rationing starts under 600. Wrigley
  Reservoir above Avalon is covered (floating cover) and is the treated-water store for the south end. Haypress and
  Patrick reservoirs are off the supply and left for wildlife; Cape Canyon, Buffalo Springs and the Buffalo Corral
  reservoirs are ranch-era stock dams, many holding water all year. Echo Lake is the only natural lake. Surface
  water persists all year in parts of Bullrush, Cottonwood and Middle canyons and the Ben Weston stream; about 35
  ponds and reservoirs cover 80 acres in total. Torqua Spring, Big Springs and Little Springs canyons are named
  for their water.
- **Land:** the Conservancy owns 88% (42,000 acres); the Catalina Island Company about 4,000 acres including
  Two Harbors and El Rancho Escondido; Avalon 1,800 acres in 1,409 parcels; USC 14 acres; SCE 21 acres. Camps
  lease their coves. Middle Ranch is the Conservancy's interior base: employee housing, the native plant nursery,
  shops and warehouse, a veterinary field clinic, stables.
